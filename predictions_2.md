## 2. My findings

### Finding 1
- Where: https://mgmt6110.vercel.app — homepage, the weather recommendation strip
  below the hero.
- What I did, what I saw: I landed on the homepage and read the strip. It gives
  today's conditions and three suggested pieces, but at first glance nothing tells a
  visitor that these suggestions come from Singapore's live weather and change with
  it. Someone arriving for the first time would not understand why those three pieces
  are being shown.
- Which heuristic: 1 — Visibility of System Status.
- Screen or system: Screen. The strip already receives everything it needs from
  `/api/weather`; it is how the screen presents it that does not say the suggestions
  are live.
- Severity, and why: 2 — how often it happens (frequency): every visitor who lands on
  the homepage passes this strip, which is what keeps it on the list, but nothing is
  lost when they miss the point, so it stays a minor problem.
- The repair: A first-time visitor can tell at a glance that these suggestions come
  from today's live Singapore weather and change with it — through the wording, colour
  or a small live indicator.

### Finding 2
- Where: https://mgmt6110.vercel.app — homepage, Curated Silhouette Gallery, reached
  by tapping Explore Collection. Phone Chrome.
- What I did, what I saw: On my phone the site looks right, but in the catalogue every
  outfit comes one at a time, each filling the screen. On my laptop I can see five or
  six outfits at once as I scroll. On the phone I have to scroll through them one by
  one, which makes browsing the collection slow and tiring, and a visitor may give up
  before seeing the range.
- Which heuristic: 6 — Recognition Rather than Recall.
- Screen or system: Screen. The gallery already has every product it needs; this is
  the phone layout showing one card per row where it could show several.
- Severity, and why: 3 — what it costs when it does (impact): a phone visitor has to
  scroll through the collection one outfit at a time, and may give up before seeing
  the range, so the cost is losing the visitor rather than just slowing them down.
- The repair: On a phone the gallery shows several pieces at a time, so a visitor can
  see the range as they scroll rather than one outfit per screen.

### Finding 3
- Where: https://mgmt6110.vercel.app — product detail screen, reached from the
  gallery. Phone Safari, phone Chrome, a second phone, and laptop.
- What I did, what I saw: I opened a product from the gallery and pressed the
  browser's back button to go back to the collection. Nothing happened — the screen
  did not change and the browser did not respond. The same on both my phones and on my
  laptop, in more than one browser. The website's own "Back to Discover & Shop" link
  works properly; only the browser's back button does not.
- Which heuristic: 3 — User Control and Freedom.
- Screen or system: System. The product screen cannot fix this on its own. The back
  button follows the browser's own history, which is set by the way the whole site
  handles moving between screens, not by anything on the product screen. That every
  browser and every device behaves the same way confirms it is the system.
- Severity, and why: 3 — how often it happens (frequency): every visitor who opens a
  product meets this, on every device and browser I tried, and it is that frequency
  that turns it from a small annoyance into a poor experience for everyone.
- The repair: Pressing the browser's back button takes the visitor back to the
  collection they came from, on every device and browser, with their place kept.

### Finding 4
- Where: https://mgmt6110.vercel.app — homepage, Special Occasions banner ("Custom
  outfits for special occasions from S$150").
- What I did, what I saw: I read the custom outfits offer. The price is clear and it
  says custom pieces can be made for different occasions, but there is no way to act
  on it. The banner is not clickable, it does not lead anywhere, and nothing on it
  says whom to contact or where to place a custom order. The only contact address,
  hello@snuffers.com, is further down in the footer and is not connected to this offer.
- Which heuristic: 10 — Help and Documentation.
- Screen or system: Screen. The fix lives entirely in the interface — making the
  banner clickable and giving it a destination that shows the contact details. No new
  route or data is needed from the back end.
- Severity, and why: 3 — what it costs when it does (impact): a visitor who wants a
  custom outfit cannot get one, because the banner is not clickable, does not lead
  anywhere and does not say whom to contact, so the shop loses a customer who had
  already decided to buy.
- The repair: A visitor who wants a custom outfit can click the Special Occasions
  banner and reach a page telling them exactly how to order one — an email address and
  a WhatsApp contact — so that nobody who wants a custom piece is left without a next
  step.

## 3. My predictions

1. The three findings I expect my groupmates to raise, and the severity I expect them
   to give each:

   - Finding 1, the weather strip that does not read as live. I expect severity 2. It
     is easy to see because it sits directly below the brand tagline and the hero
     image, before the catalogue begins, so it is the first thing a visitor meets
     after the hero. Anyone looking at the site properly will either understand what
     it is doing or notice that they do not.

   - Finding 3, the dead browser back button. I expect severity 3. It is easy to see
     because a groupmate browsing the collection will open a product, look at it, and
     then press the browser's back arrow to return — and nothing will happen.

   - Finding 4, the custom outfits banner that is not clickable. I expect severity 2.
     It is easy to see because the banner is a different colour from the rest of the
     page and stands out, so a groupmate is likely to be curious about where it leads
     and try to click it, only to find that it does not go anywhere.

2. The heuristic I think my product breaks worst: 1 — Visibility of System Status.
   Even though the strip says "Cloudy in the City" and I know that means it is reading
   Singapore's live weather and choosing the outfits from it, a visitor who does not
   know that could easily read it as something random, or as decoration put there for
   show. The product's most distinctive feature depends on the visitor understanding
   that something live is happening, and the screen does not make that clear.

3. The finding that would show my own evaluation was wrong: if a groupmate reports
   that their first visit after several hours shows stale or missing weather
   information, under heuristic 1 at severity 3 or higher, my evaluation was wrong. I
   would have missed it because I tested the site repeatedly within short intervals,
   when the cached answer was always fresh, and never checked what the first visit
   after a long gap looks like.
