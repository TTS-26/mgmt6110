# REFLECTION


This reflection describes my experience developing Snuffers and what I learned about using AI as a collaborator throughout the design and development process.

---

## 1. What did I originally intend to build, and what did I actually build?

My original idea for Snuffers was to build a chic yet attainable fashion brand for dogs. I wanted dog owners to be able to style their dogs according to their preferences and budget, while also having access to fashionable outfits for different occasions. The broader idea was to create a convenient destination where dog owners could find stylish clothing instead of having to look across different places for different products.

As I started developing the concept, I realised that the idea needed to become more focused. I began thinking more carefully about which dogs the platform was really serving and what problem I wanted to solve. I noticed that fashionable dog clothing in the market often appears to be more focused on smaller dogs, while larger dogs and dogs with different body shapes may have fewer opportunities to find equally fashionable clothing.

This led me to make inclusivity a much stronger part of the Snuffers concept. The final product focuses on providing chic and attainable designs for dogs of different breeds, sizes, and body shapes. Clothing became the clear core of the platform, while shoes and treats were kept as small secondary categories. I included only three shoe products and a small treat section so that these products could complement the main fashion offering without taking attention away from the core user job.

The final prototype therefore became much more focused than my original broader idea. Instead of trying to represent an entire commercial marketplace, I concentrated on helping a dog owner discover a stylish outfit that will actually fit their dog.

---

## 2. How did I use AI, and what did I learn about working with AI?

I used AI throughout different stages of the project, but I did not use it in exactly the same way at each stage.

At the beginning, AI was particularly useful for idea generation and brainstorming. I started by explaining my overall idea and then used AI to explore different possibilities. I could respond to its suggestions with my own ideas, and this created an iterative brainstorming process that helped me refine the concept and identify a clearer goal.

AI was also very useful in helping me structure my ideas into a detailed master prompt. I learned that the quality and specificity of the instructions given to AI have a major impact on the output. The more clearly I explained the target user, core job, screens, features, visual direction, sizing requirements, and limitations, the easier it became to guide the tools towards the intended result.

I then used Google Stitch to translate the specification into the visual design and front-end direction. The ability to make smaller refinements without completely redesigning the existing concept was particularly useful. Google AI Studio was then used to turn the selected design into a functional frontend prototype.

Overall, I learned that AI works much better as an iterative collaborator when the human provides clear direction, constraints, and feedback rather than expecting one prompt to produce a perfect final result.

---

## 3. What went wrong or surprised me during the process, and how did I respond?

One of the biggest things that surprised me was how much impact even a small amount of vague wording could have on the final output. I initially assumed that if I explained the overall idea clearly, the AI would understand the intended direction. However, I learned that AI can interpret vague instructions in ways that are quite different from what I intended.

For example, the initial Google AI Studio build changed parts of the layout and visual hierarchy and introduced generic or unrelated imagery. It showed me that even when the original design direction was strong, the implementation could still move away from it. Instead of starting over, I used a focused correction prompt and specifically instructed AI not to redesign or change elements that were already working.

I also noticed that AI sometimes introduced elements that were not part of my requirements. This made it important to explicitly state what should not be added, such as search, additional screens, checkout, backend functionality, or unnecessary workflows.

The process taught me that human intervention is important throughout the entire development process. I could not simply provide an initial prompt and assume that every subsequent output would remain aligned with the original intention. I had to continuously inspect the results, identify unintended changes, and give more precise instructions.

---

## 4. What did I learn about my own decision-making and the role of human judgement?

The biggest lesson I learned was that human judgement remains extremely important even when AI is capable of generating designs and making changes.

At several points, I used AI to help me think through design decisions. However, when I presented different options and asked which one was better, AI often explained the strengths and weaknesses of both options rather than making a clear decision. This showed me that AI can be very useful for generating alternatives and analysing possibilities, but I still need to decide which option is most appropriate for my specific objective.

I also had to make decisions about when an AI-generated element was unnecessary, when a design was moving away from the core user job, and when something needed to be corrected. This required me to continuously compare the output against the original requirements.

I also manually tested the final prototype to check whether the important interactions worked as intended. I checked the category filters, chest-girth filtering, product-detail navigation, matching-size highlighting, size and colour selection, and the Add to Bag interaction. This reinforced the idea that AI-generated output still needs human evaluation and testing.

My role was therefore not just to give instructions to AI. I had to act as the person responsible for judging whether the output actually solved the problem.

---

## 5. What would I do differently if I did this again?

If I were to do this project again, I would start with a more focused concept from the beginning. I think defining the target user, core problem, and product scope earlier would reduce unnecessary iteration and allow me to move through the design and development process faster.

I would also test AI-generated outputs earlier rather than assuming that a detailed prompt would automatically produce the intended result. I now understand that working with AI is an iterative process where prompting, reviewing, correcting, and testing all need to happen together.

Overall, the project changed how I think about AI. I see it less as a tool that independently produces the final answer and more as a collaborator that can generate ideas and execute directions, while the human remains responsible for setting the direction, evaluating the results, correcting mistakes, and making the final decisions.
